\begin{verbatimwrite}{Cagetti2005tk-Abstract.txt}
  Cagetti and De Nardi (2006) study how entrepreneurship, borrowing constraints, and bequest motives help explain the high concentration of wealth in the United States. Their model combines a life-cycle household problem with occupational choice: households can work for wages or operate a business, and entrepreneurs choose both saving and business capital while facing an enforcement constraint on borrowing. The original paper calibrates this environment to match key wealth and entrepreneurship moments and finds that entrepreneurs are central for explaining top wealth shares, while tighter borrowing constraints reduce both inequality and aggregate entrepreneurial activity. In this reproduction paper, I focus on the theoretical model rather than the SCF data work or the full general equilibrium calibration. I restate the household problem, decompose the worker and entrepreneur branches, run the existing theory notebook, and compare the notebook's outputs with the benchmark results from the paper.
\end{verbatimwrite}
